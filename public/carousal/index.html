Solar System

Scroll through the neighborhood around our star.

Star

Sun

The Sun holds more than 99 percent of the solar system's mass. Its gravity keeps every planet, dwarf planet, asteroid, and comet moving through space.

  • Surface temperature: about 5,500 C
  • Light reaches Earth in about 8 minutes

Innermost planet

Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and races around the Sun faster than any other world. With almost no atmosphere, its days swing between extreme heat and deep cold.

  • Year length: 88 Earth days
  • No moons

Hottest planet

Venus

Venus is wrapped in thick clouds of carbon dioxide and sulfuric acid. Its runaway greenhouse effect makes it hotter than Mercury, even though it sits farther from the Sun.

  • Average surface temperature: about 465 C
  • Rotates backward compared with most planets

Our home

Earth

Earth is the only known world with liquid oceans on the surface and life. Its magnetic field and atmosphere help shield the planet from solar radiation.

  • One moon
  • Surface is about 71 percent water

Earth's companion

Moon

The Moon stabilizes Earth's tilt and shapes ocean tides. Its cratered surface preserves a long record of impacts from the early solar system.

  • Average distance from Earth: about 384,400 km
  • Always shows Earth roughly the same face

The red planet

Mars

Mars is a cold desert world with dusty skies, polar ice caps, and evidence of ancient rivers and lakes. Robotic explorers continue to search for signs of past habitability.

  • Two small moons: Phobos and Deimos
  • Home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system

Gas giant

Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet, a massive world of hydrogen and helium. Its Great Red Spot is a giant storm that has lasted for centuries.

  • More than 90 known moons
  • About 11 Earths wide

Ringed giant

Saturn

Saturn's bright rings are made of countless icy particles, from tiny grains to large chunks. The planet itself is so low in density it would float in a huge enough ocean.

  • Famous moon: Titan
  • Day length: about 10.7 hours

Ice giant

Uranus

Uranus rotates on its side, giving it extreme seasons that last for decades. Methane in its atmosphere gives the planet its pale blue-green color.

  • Axial tilt: about 98 degrees
  • Has faint rings

Farthest planet

Neptune

Neptune is dark, cold, and whipped by supersonic winds. Its deep blue color comes from methane and high-altitude haze in the atmosphere.

  • Year length: about 165 Earth years
  • Largest moon: Triton
